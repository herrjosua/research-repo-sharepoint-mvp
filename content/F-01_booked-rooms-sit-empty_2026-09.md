*Fictional demo data.*

## Finding
At Harborview Group headquarters, 5 of 6 people interviewed had walked up to a room that RoomBook
showed as booked and found it empty. The usage data agrees: about 31% of booked hours went unused
over two weeks.

## What people said
- P01, office manager: "I get a message a day: it says booked, it's empty, can you cancel it? I can
  cancel anything. I just never know if I should."
- P03, software engineer: "Booked rooms are a suggestion."
- P04, sales representative: "I showed up with a client, the room was booked and empty, so we just
  sat down. Nobody came."

## How people work around it
- **Look in first, then take it.** P02 and P03 walk over and check the window. P02: "If I peek in
  and it's empty at five past, I take it." P04 also takes an empty room.
- **Ask the booker.** P03 and P05 ping the person who booked it in Teams.
- **Use the door tablet.** P05 checks the floor 3 tablet, which shows live occupancy: "It's the only
  thing I trust." Only floor 3 has tablets.
- **Cancel by guessing.** P01 walks both floors mid-morning and cancels bookings that look
  abandoned, knowing it is a guess.

## Evidence
- N-01 interview notes: P01, P02, P03, P04, P05 (P06 had not yet seen it, three weeks into the job).
- A-01 usage summary: 109 of 352 booked hours unused (31.0%); 78 of 268 bookings (29.1%) were
  no-shows, meaning no one was detected in the first 15 minutes.

## Limits
Six interviews at one site show that the problem exists and how people cope, not how common it is
across about 400 staff. The usage data shows that rooms go unused, not why.
