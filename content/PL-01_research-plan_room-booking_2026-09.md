*Fictional demo data. Harborview Group, RoomBook and all people in this record are invented.*

## Background
Harborview Group is a fictional company with about 400 people at one headquarters site. Staff book
the site's 8 meeting rooms (4 on floor 2, 4 on floor 3) in RoomBook, an internal web app that is
separate from the Outlook calendar. The office manager reported a steady stream of complaints that
rooms look booked but are empty. This study looks at what people struggle with when booking a room
and what they do about it.

## Research questions
1. What are the main pain points people have when booking a meeting room?
2. How do people work around each pain point?
3. What did we not expect to learn?

## Method
- **Interviews:** 6 semi-structured interviews, remote, 30 to 45 minutes each, one participant per
  session, run by Maya Alvarez. Notes are in N-01.
- **Usage data:** a pull of RoomBook bookings and door-sensor occupancy for all 8 rooms, 2026-09-08
  to 2026-09-19 (10 working days). Summarized in A-01.

## Participants
Six employees, chosen to cover different ways of using the rooms: an office manager, a project
manager, a software engineer, a sales representative who is in the office two days a week, an
executive assistant who books for three executives, and a new hire three weeks into the job.

## Out of scope
Room equipment faults, catering, visitor access, and any room outside the headquarters site.

## What this study can and cannot answer
Six interviews show what kinds of problems exist and how people cope. They do not show how common
each problem is across all 400 staff. The usage data shows how rooms were used, not why.
