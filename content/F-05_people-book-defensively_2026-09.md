## Finding
This was the unexpected result. We went in expecting people to describe other people's empty
bookings (F-01). We did not expect them to describe their own: 4 of 6 people interviewed (P02, P03,
P05, P06) said they book rooms they might not need, because they do not expect to find one later. P01, the office manager, said "everyone" does it. Their own
bookings are part of why rooms look booked but sit empty.

## What people said
- P02, project manager: "I hold the room until I know I don't need it. Letting go is risky."
- P03, software engineer: "I book a four-person room as backup. If the good one falls through, I
  have somewhere."
- P05, executive assistant: "If I cancel, I lose it. Someone grabs it in minutes."
- P06, new hire: "My buddy told me rooms are impossible to get, so I book two and cancel one." This
  was learned from a colleague in the first weeks on the job.

## Supporting data
Small rooms are the most affected. In A-01, the three 4-seat rooms had 40.4% of booked hours unused,
against 30.0% for 6-seat rooms and 25.0% for the larger rooms. This is consistent with P03's backup
booking of small rooms but does not prove it.

## Our interpretation
This is our reading and is not something participants said. Empty-but-booked rooms (F-01) make
people distrust availability, so they book defensively, which creates more empty-but-booked rooms.
Recurring bookings that cannot be released one date at a time (F-04) feed the same loop. We did not
test this.

## Limits
Six interviews at one site. Whether defensive booking is the main cause of the unused hours is not
established.
